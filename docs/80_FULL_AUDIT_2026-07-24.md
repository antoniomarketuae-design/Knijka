## 1. Executive verdict

**Is it launchable? Not today. It is roughly 90 focused hours from launchable, and none of those 90 hours are architectural.**

That sentence is the whole audit. The hard parts of this product — the parts that take a year and cannot be bought — are built, tested and working. The parts that are missing are almost entirely *commercial plumbing and legal identity*: a registered entity, a working mailbox, a password reset, an entitlement check on two routes, a deploy that can roll back.

What is genuinely rare here, and what must be protected:

- **A real learning engine, not a progress bar.** SM-2-lite scheduling with an explicit anti-gaming rule, asymptotic mastery gains weighted by question difficulty, multiplicative decay on error, coverage-penalised readiness. `modules/learning/scheduler.ts:44-47` refuses to extend an interval when a student answers correctly *before* a review is due — that is a deliberate refusal to let drilling inflate the number, and most ed-tech does the opposite.
- **Theory and simulator are one learner model, wired both ways.** Sim violations fold into the same `Progress` rows as practice, severity-weighted to mirror the official 10/3/1 hierarchy (`modules/learning/simFeed.ts`), and a mid-drive micro-quiz feeds back the other direction. Very few competitors anywhere will have this loop.
- **A rule engine that knows what it cannot judge.** 27 sustain-based detectors, a 1,750-line false-positive battery with ~120 named innocent-driving cases, ~145 recorded-trace regressions, and a detector (SP-06) that was *removed* with the reasoning recorded because the telemetry could not distinguish caution from obstruction. That restraint is the reason the sim's verdicts could one day be trusted by a parent or a school.
- **A deterministic trace format plus a headless GPU render pipeline.** One shape serves the ghost, the mistake demo, the student's attempt and the offline film renderer, with an explicit law that a trace is *recorded kinematics, never re-simulated physics*. A competitor can copy the question bank in a quarter; copying this takes 6–18 months.
- **Provable exam-format fidelity.** 500/500 generated exams hit exactly 45 unique questions and exactly 97 points, with a suffix-feasibility DP guaranteeing the point solver cannot get stuck. Timing and grading are server-authoritative and tamper-proof.
- **Structural ADR enforcement.** Zero real-brand vehicle strings anywhere in `src/`. `@anthropic-ai/sdk` imported in exactly **one** file (`modules/tutor/model.ts`). ADR-001 and ADR-002 are not policy documents here — they are single chokepoints.

And what is honestly wrong:

- **The company does not exist yet.** The data-controller identity, ЕИК, address and the *only* support/GDPR/password-recovery channel are literal placeholder strings rendered to users today, while Stripe needs only two env vars to start charging minors.
- **The paywall does not gate what the packs are sold on.** The €21.99 tier's entire delta over €12.99 is a checkmark on the pricing page.
- **The content bank has never been reviewed by a human.** Zero of 1,089 questions are `approved` in 246 commits, and unreviewed drafts feed the mock exam.
- **The bank leaks its own answers.** 76–81% of single-answer questions are solvable by picking the longest option; one topic file has the correct answer at position (a) in 37 of 37 questions.
- **The rule engine punishes correct driving in four verified places.** The most damaging class of error a driving product can make.
- **Nobody has ever measured the product on a phone.** ~9.5 MB and 5.4 MB of JavaScript to start the simulator, and a 1.1 MB PNG poster on every mistake reel.

**Composite score: 5.25 / 10** — the score of a technically strong, commercially unfinished product.

## 3. Critical findings — launch blockers

These four block taking money. Nothing else on this list does.

### C-1 · Legal identity, support channel and GDPR rights are placeholder strings
**Dimensions:** security, UX, business · **Effort: 3h (+10h for the rights tooling, C-2)**

`platform/src/app/(legal)/legal-ui.tsx:20-24` exports five literal placeholders — `ENTITY_NAME="[ИМЕ НА ЮРИДИЧЕСКО ЛИЦЕ]"`, `ENTITY_EIK="[ЕИК]"`, `ENTITY_ADDRESS="[АДРЕС]"`, `CONTACT_EMAIL="[ИМЕЙЛ ЗА КОНТАКТ]"`, `LAST_UPDATED="[ДАТА]"`. They render verbatim in 12 places across `/privacy`, `/terms` and `/contact` (`OperatorCard` at `legal-ui.tsx:194-198`, `contact/page.tsx:31`).

Every user remedy terminates on that string: account deletion (`settings/page.tsx:89-92`), data export (`:84-88`), password recovery (`login/page.tsx:31-39`, `settings/page.tsx:64-74`), parental requests (`contact/page.tsx:52-55`).

Two escalating facts:
1. **Payments need two env vars, not a Stripe dashboard.** `modules/payments/checkout.ts:54,105` builds `line_items` with inline `price_data`, so no product setup is required. `isStripeConfigured()` (`stripe.ts:37`) is the only gate. Someone sets `STRIPE_SECRET_KEY` on the VPS and the app begins charging minors under a policy naming "[ИМЕ НА ЮРИДИЧЕСКО ЛИЦЕ]" — with no error, no test failure, no CI red.
2. **Consent is already being collected today, on staging, with no Stripe key.** `register-form.tsx:220-238` requires an explicit GDPR consent checkbox linking to `/privacy` and `/terms`. GDPR Art. 13(1)(a)/(b) controller identity and contact details are therefore missing *at the moment of consent collection, from minors*. The consent is arguably invalid on its face.

Mitigating: a `DraftBanner` (`legal-ui.tsx:31-40`) labels the legal pages as pre-launch, staging is tunnel-only, and this is already item #1 in `docs/legal/50` and `[HIGH]` in `docs/platform/75`. Present-tense harm is near zero; the harm is entirely conditional on launch — which is exactly why it needs a *guard*, not a checklist line.

**Fix.** Register the entity, fill the five constants, provision the mailbox. Then add the thing that is actually missing: a vitest assertion that fails if any exported legal constant matches `/^\[.*\]$/`, plus a throw in `isStripeConfigured()` when placeholders are still bracketed. Converts a markdown checklist item into something that cannot be forgotten.

### C-2 · No account-deletion or data-export code path exists
**Dimension:** security · **Effort: 10h**

A grep for `user.delete|deleteUser|deleteAccount|exportData` across `platform/src` returns nothing outside generated Prisma code. The settings "Изтрий акаунта ми — заявка" control is a `<Link href="/contact">`.

The schema is ready and waiting — every child model carries `onDelete: Cascade` (Progress, QuestionAttempt, ExamAttempt, SimSession, Entitlement, GamificationState, TutorThread). Nothing calls it.

**Honest severity note.** GDPR does not require self-service tooling; a one-person operator may lawfully fulfil requests by hand within one month, and `docs/platform/75` item B1 deliberately scoped this as "mailto or server action". What makes it critical is not the missing tooling — it is that the request channel itself (C-1) does not exist, so the rights pipeline is not merely manual, it is **unaddressable**.

**Fix.** Two server actions behind `requireUser()`: `exportMyData()` (User minus passwordHash plus all child rows as a JSON download) and `deleteMyAccount()` (password re-entry → `db.user.delete`, cascades do the rest, retain only invoice facts as `privacy/page.tsx:167-172` already states). ~150 lines.

### C-3 · The two headline paid features are not gated — both packs are unsellable as described
**Dimensions:** business, UX · **Effort: 6h**

`getEntitlements` / `hasPremium` appear in the entire app only at `pricing/page.tsx:57,84` and `ComparisonTable.tsx:107`. `simulator/page.tsx:60` calls only `requireUser()`. `tutor/page.tsx:19` and `tutor/actions.ts:17` likewise. The code says so itself — `components/payments/ComparisonTable.tsx:51-54`:

> `// TODO(founder): /simulator в момента НЯМА entitlement гейт — и безплатен акаунт може да кара.`

Meanwhile `modules/tutor/service.ts:21` gives **every** account `TUTOR_DAILY_MESSAGE_LIMIT = 30` per day forever, while the table advertises free tutor as "кратък пробен достъп".

So: the €12.99 core pack is marketed on "AI Учител — пълен достъп" (already free) and the €21.99 premium pack on "Шофьорски симулатор — пълен достъп" (already free). **`hasPremium` is consumed at exactly one site in the codebase — to render an "owned" badge.** The total delivered value of the €9 upsell is a checkmark.

The only real paywall is unlimited practice (20/day free) and unlimited mock exams (1 free) — `quota.ts:28,31`, wired at `theory/practice/actions.ts:51` and `exams/actions.ts:60`. That machinery is correct and thoughtful (Europe/Sofia calendar-day boundaries with a two-step DST fixpoint; started attempts count as used so exams cannot be farmed).

Two mitigations that keep this a launch blocker rather than a live liability: the free exam cap is production-only (`exams/actions.ts:59`), so it is invisible on staging; and buy buttons only render when `isStripeConfigured()`, so no money has been taken on the false bullets.

**Fix.** Pick one and ship it in the same commit as the copy: either add `getEntitlements(user.id).hasPremium` to `/simulator` (with L0 free as the demo) and a lifetime free-message counter to the tutor, or move those rows to free and re-justify the premium tier with something else. Do not ship with the TODO.

### C-4 · Graded Б2 "STOP" lines sit under visible Б1 give-way signs — legal rolling entry = instant exam fail
**Dimension:** rule engine · **Effort: 4h**

Two heuristics disagree about the same junction.

- `modules/sim/runtime/stoplines.ts:229-240` derives a graded line at any unsignalized node where some incident edge has `classRank>=4`, on approaches with `rank<=2`, and **always** with `control = "stopSign"` (the file's own comment at line 78 admits the heuristic never manufactures a `giveWay`).
- `modules/sim/world/builders/props.ts:266-273` paints the **visible** sign with a different rule: `const kind: SignKind = maxRank >= 5 ? "stop" : "giveWay"`.

Replaying both over the real `content/world/district-v1.json` (323 edges, 112 intersections) yields 14 graded stop lines, of which **4 sit under a painted Б1 give-way triangle**: `n316056951/e1182196532.0` (ул. Дъбница), `n4372628948/e519275129.0`, `n5063751788/e1375487707.0`, `n9601848047/e1043264868.0`.

`rules/engine.ts:1380-1389` then grades a non-stop crossing as `STOP_SIGN_NO_FULL_STOP` = опасна, 10 points, and `lessons/exam.ts:45` terminates the exam on any опасна.

**Reach is worse than it looks.** `n5063751788` is on the *shipped practical-exam route*, traversed twice per lap by exam Shells A and B — and `examBankData.ts:459` documents it as "the Б2-guarded n5063751788", i.e. the author believed a Б2 was there and the world builder contradicts him.

A student sees "Пропусни движението", correctly rolls through a clear mouth — the exact behaviour the product's own `sc-jx-giveway-b1` lesson teaches — and is instant-failed at maximum severity, contradicting the sign in front of their eyes. This is the single most credibility-destroying class of error a driving product can commit.

Separately, 8 of the 14 graded lines sit on `service` edges (parking aisles, driveways) where no Б2 exists in reality — a map-realism problem rather than a contradiction, but worth the same patch.

**Fix.** Make `stoplines.ts` derive `control` with the *same* rule `props.ts` uses; add a cross-check test asserting that for every shipped district, every graded line's control equals the painted sign kind on that approach; exclude `service` edges from graded priority lines.

## 4. High findings

Grouped and deduplicated. Each is real, reproduced, and not launch-blocking on its own.

### The content bank leaks its own answers (H-1 … H-3)

**H-1 · The correct answer is guessable from option length, and practice never shuffles. Effort: 60h (25h to instrument + a bank pass)**
Across 698 single-answer questions, the longest option is correct **76–81%** of the time (81.4% counting ties, 76.1% requiring a unique longest) against a 25% chance baseline. Mean correct-option length 70.4 chars vs 44.0 for distractors (ratio 1.60). Practice preserves stored order verbatim (`theory/practice/page.tsx:85`; `PracticeSession.tsx:284`) — only the exam shuffles (`builder.ts:167`), and shuffling positions does nothing to a length tell.

*Corrections to the first-pass claim, both important:* a longest-option bot scores **45.3% of points** on the exam-eligible pool, not 51.3%, because `submit.ts:120` enforces exact-set matching and the bot scores **0/274 on multi questions**. Random exact-set guessing scores 17.0%. So the honest lift is ~2.7× chance, and **the tell alone cannot pass a 87/97 exam**. It also cannot "teach test-craft instead of driving" as an observed harm — there are no users yet.

What *is* mechanically certain and serious: `correct` is the sole input to mastery, lapses, reps and `dueAt` (`submit.ts:82-83`), so any answering strategy correlated with option length measurably corrupts the adaptive signal — and therefore the readiness number the whole product sells. It is also getting worse: first-20-per-file (the audited wave) 73.9%, everything generated after 84.4%.

The project's own verifier is reverse-fitted and mostly blind to it: `tools/theory/verify_drafts.mjs:37-41` sets `STRONG_TELL_RATIO=2.2` with the comment "Tuned on the live bank … flags ~52 drafts". Of the 568 exploitable singles it catches **13.9%** at the blocking tier, 36.6% including the info tier. 63% produce no signal at all.

**Fix:** (a) seed-shuffle options in the practice DTO, per (user, question), stable within a session; (b) re-tune the tell thresholds to ~1.35 ratio / 15 chars and add a per-file gate; (c) rewrite distractors to length parity (±20% of the question's mean option length) and make that a generation rule. (a) and (b) are hours; (c) is the 60h.

**H-2 · Answer-position bias up to 100% per topic file, with no position check anywhere. Effort: 4h**
Correct answer at option (a) for single questions: 318/698 = 45.6% overall (chance 25%), distribution `{a:318, b:180, c:122, d:78}` — a monotone decay, not noise. Per file: `dokumenti-i-sanktsii.json` **37/37 = 100%**, `prevozno-sredstvo` 92%, `manevri-i-izprevarvane` 87%, `osnovni-ponyatia` 80%, `alkohol-i-godnost` 72%. For 4-option/3-correct multis, the lone wrong option sits at (d) in 120/223 = 53.8%.

This was diagnosed and hand-fixed once (`content/audits/predimstvo.audit.json`, 2026-07-07: "Main defect fixed: severe answer-position bias") and then **regenerated by the next wave** — `content/audits/eko-i-zashtitno-shofirane.audit.json` records the same defect being hand-fixed a second time. Audit coverage totals 339 of 1,089 questions. Post-audit slices regressed from 39.9% to 47.9% at (a).

`verify_drafts.mjs` has no position check of any kind (enumerated every emitted code: structural, concept-unresolved, dead-law-ref, uncertain-law-ref, option-dup-text, correct-length-tell, mild-length-tell, trivial-explanation). And `verify_drafts.mjs` **is not in CI at all** — `.github/workflows/ci.yml` runs tsc, vitest, sim-harness, build.

*Correction:* the mock exam already shuffles options (`builder.ts:167`), so exam-day exposure is de-biased. The unmitigated harm is one layer earlier — practice renders stored order, and `mastery.ts` raises mastery on every correct answer, so a student guessing "a" drives `dokumenti-i-sanktsii` mastery asymptotically to 1 without learning anything. **The bias is invisible to the metric meant to catch it.**

**Fix:** a `position-bias` check in `verify_drafts.mjs` failing any file above ~35% at (a), plus a one-time seeded option-shuffle across all 1,089 items preserving option ids and `correct` flags. **Highest value-per-hour fix in the entire bank** — fully automatable and permanently closes the regression.

**H-3 · 345 questions ship a self-flagged uncertain law citation, rendered to the learner as authority. Effort: 24h**
370 `lawRef` entries contain a literal `?` in the article reference across **345 distinct questions (31.7% of the bank)** — `ЗДвП чл. 151?`, `Кодекс за застраховането чл. 557?`. 34 more leak `чл. N?` into the explanation prose. These render verbatim as citation chips (`WhyPanel.tsx:117-119`, `ExamResultView.tsx:157-166`, `MicroQuizOverlay.tsx:195`).

57 of the 345 are `draft`, and `draft` is exam-eligible (`builder.ts:62`) — with **60% of the signs exam pool affected** (70 eligible / 42 with `?`), and `builder.ts:190-216` guaranteeing every non-empty topic at least one slot, the great majority of generated mock exams contain at least one `?`-cited question. This is not a rare tail.

**Worse sibling defect the first pass nearly missed:** 58 questions ship a literal internal QA note as the *leading text* of `explanationBg` — e.g. `q-signs-027`: `[REVIEW: да се потвърди точният член от ЗДвП за задълженията пред жп прелез — чл. 51–52?] Б2 значи пълно спиране...`. A stripper exists (`modules/content-admin/logic.ts:26-30`, `stripReviewPrefix`) but runs **only inside the founder approval tool**, never on the learner render path (`modules/learning/whyPanel.ts:328` passes `explanationBg` verbatim). Practice serves `needs-review` by default (`session.ts:86,122`), so students see staff notes admitting the platform is unsure the law it just taught is current.

**Fix:** (a) one-line reuse — call `stripReviewNote()` at the read boundary in `lib/content/loader.ts` (**1h, highest ratio in this section**); (b) a load-time + validate-content rule rejecting any `lawRef` matching `/[?]/`, turning the 345 into a hard queue; (c) resolve each against the actual ЗДвП/ППЗДвП text.

### The bank has never been reviewed (H-4)

**H-4 · Zero questions have ever been approved, yet 799 unreviewed drafts feed live mock exams. Effort: 30h founder editorial**
Status census: `{draft: 799, needs-review: 290, approved: 0}`. `git log --all -S'"status": "approved"' -- content/questions/` is **empty across all 246 commits**. `verify_drafts.mjs` live: "draft questions: 799 (clean 586 / flagged 213)". Both consumption paths treat `draft` as shippable: `builder.ts:61-63` and `session.ts:121`.

*Two corrections.* "Code inverts SCHEMA.md" is overstated — `SCHEMA.md:143` Hard Rule 2 defines `needs-review` as the quarantine, and `builder.ts:1-8` documents the policy deliberately. And "nobody is watching" is false: there is a read-only verifier, a generated per-topic review queue (`docs/development/65`, ~542 min estimated), a `review_batch.mjs` that refuses non-draft and flagged ids, and an open backlog item. The gap is known, quantified, tooled and tracked.

What remains unambiguous: **the approval workflow exists end-to-end and has never once been executed.** Of the 213 flagged drafts, the substantive classes are `dead-law-ref` (149) and `uncertain-law-ref` (58) — and those 58 are, by SCHEMA.md's own Hard Rule 2, mis-filed into the exam-eligible pool.

**Fix:** drive the 213 flagged drafts to zero, approve at minimum the exam sampling surface, then add a CI assertion narrowing `isExamEligible` to `approved` (or at minimum excluding any draft with a `?`-suffixed lawRef) before the first paying user.

### The rule engine punishes correct driving (H-5 … H-6)

**H-5 · A textbook right lane change inside a crossing zone is simultaneously commended and instant-failed. Effort: 3h**
`rules/engine.ts:531-538` fires `OVERTAKING_AT_CROSSING` (опасна, 10 pts) on **any** lane-id delta — either direction — while a crossing zone is armed and a lead is within 45 m. The `dir` variable computed one block above (line 505) is never referenced. Unlike the Wave-3 detectors, OV-07 ships default-ON.

Probe returned exactly `["SAFE_LANE_CHANGE","OVERTAKING_AT_CROSSING"]`. Reachable at 28 of district-v1's 54 crossings (those on edges with ≥2 lanes per direction). The cleanest real-world false positive is not merging back to the curb — it is the legally *required* move into the rightmost lane to prepare a right turn at a junction with a car queued ahead. Crossings arm 35 m out, i.e. exactly there.

*Correction:* the co-occurrence with `SAFE_LANE_CHANGE` is deliberate and test-asserted ("distinct acts") — an examiner genuinely does say "you signalled properly, but you may not overtake here". The bug is the direction blindness, not the co-occurrence.

**Worse, and missed by the first pass: `s.crossing` is sticky.** The engine clears it only on a matching `crossingPassed` (`engine.ts:1417-1431`), but `runtime/zones.ts:96-102` splices a zone out of `active` at >38 m **with no exit event** when the driver turns away instead of crossing. Probe: arm a zone, drive 60 s without passing it, then make a signalled, mirrored lane change with a 30 m lead → `OVERTAKING_AT_CROSSING`. The 35 m zone is effectively unbounded in time.

**Fix:** add a direction gate (`dir === "left"`, or better a "the lead is now behind you" check) to the engine condition, and emit `crossingZoneExited` from `zones.ts:96-102` so `s.crossing` cannot outlive the geometry. Add both to the FP battery.

**H-6 · PEDESTRIAN_NOT_YIELDED fires for zebras the car never drove over (up to 20 m away). Effort: 6h**
`runtime/zones.ts:59-61` arms a crossing zone from the host edge **and every edge sharing a node with it**; `zones.ts:115` declares a "pass" when the crossing point goes ahead→behind with `lateral <= 22 m`. A full sweep of all 323 edges via `createWorldRuntime` produced **36 off-host-edge `crossingPassed` events, 25 of them with the car never closer than 8 m** to that zebra (measured minima 9.1 / 12.4 / 16.5 / 18.8 / 20.4 m). `engine.ts:1417-1421` turns `crossingPassed` + `pedestrianOnCrossing` straight into опасна, 10 pts; `lessons/exam.ts:45` ends the exam.

*Correction, and it makes it worse:* the live lesson/exam path uses `DEFAULT_LESSON_TRAFFIC` (`modules/sim/contracts.ts:130-134`) with **pedestrianCount: 20**, not the 8 of `DEFAULT_TRAFFIC_CONFIG`. Twenty pedestrians anchored nearest-first across 51 crossings make adjacent-edge zebras near-certainly occupied.

One partial guard exists and does not save it: `traffic/pedestrians.ts:252-257` blocks a pedestrian stepping onto a crossing within 22 m of a moving player — but `crossingGateOpen` returns early for *signalized* crossings (`:230-233`), so the worst case (player crossing on green while a side-street signalized zebra has its walk phase) is entirely unguarded. Even unsignalized, the gate only blocks stepping *on*; a pedestrian already mid-crossing stays.

Root cause is a design tension, not carelessness: 22 m is deliberately sized to cover the outermost lane of a 6-lane arterial, correct for the host edge. The bug is combining it with neighbour-edge arming.

**Fix:** gate the `crossingPassed` emission on the vehicle actually occupying the crossing's host edge/lane span, while leaving `crossingZoneEntered` on the wider adjacency set. Make `pedestrianOnCrossing` side-aware.

### The exam can be silently destroyed mid-attempt (H-7)

**H-7 · The in-progress exam page rebuilds the paper from the seed, not from the stored question ids. Effort: 3h**
`exams/[attemptId]/page.tsx:57` renders `buildExam(seed).questions`, but grading uses the ids persisted at start (`modules/exam/index.ts:147`), and `grader.ts:37-56` silently ignores answers for any other id. Flipping a single question from `needs-review` to `approved` — exactly what the founder's `/review` tool does — left only **7 of 45 questions in common** for the same seed; a perfect student then scores **20/97**.

*Correction to scope:* it is not "every restart". A restart with unchanged content, or with edits touching only wording/explanations, rebuilds an identical paper. The precise trigger is any deploy carrying an **eligibility-, points- or concept-changing** edit — above all `needs-review → approved`, the one promotion the founder's own review workflow performs — for any attempt resumed afterwards. The page's only guard (`try/catch` on `ExamError`) cannot detect a *different but valid* rebuild.

**Fix:** stop rebuilding. Expose the attempt's stored `questionIds` from the exam module and render those; keep the seed only for option shuffling. Belt-and-braces: compare rebuilt ids to stored ids and render `CannotRestoreView` on mismatch. This is the same 3h change that fixes the cross-device resume problem in M-9.

### Security and abuse (H-8 … H-9)

**H-8 · No rate limiting anywhere. Effort: 8h**
A repo-wide grep for `rate.?limit|ratelimit|throttle` across `platform/src` returns only simulator physics and toast throttling. `next.config.ts` is the 5-line scaffold. `proxy.ts:12-33` only decodes a JWT.

Three concrete exposures: (1) `POST /api/register` runs bcrypt cost 12 (~250–320 ms CPU) unauthenticated and unbounded — CPU exhaustion on a small VPS (bcryptjs chunks via `setImmediate`, so it saturates rather than freezes the loop); (2) credentials login has no attempt counter or lockout, and there is no email verification anywhere; (3) each fresh account gets 30 tutor messages/day at up to 1024 output tokens — register N accounts, get N×30 Anthropic calls against the founder's key. `modules/tutor/cost.ts` tracks spend but never enforces a ceiling.

Note the deployment twist: `docs/development/54` names Vercel as the production target with Cloudflare set to DNS-only. At launch the bcrypt limb converts from VPS DoS into an autoscaled *bill*, with no WAF in front.

**Fix:** an IP+identifier limiter in `proxy.ts` covering `/api/register`, `/api/auth/*`, `/api/checkout/embedded` and the tutor action; exponential lockout after ~5 failed logins per email; a global daily Anthropic kill-switch summing `TutorThread.costMicroUsd` (the column already exists).

**H-9 · The Terms promise two consent gates at checkout that the payment code never captures. Effort: 6h**
`terms/page.tsx:179-180` requires parental consent for under-18 purchases; `:203-212` conditions instant access on an explicit waiver of the 14-day distance-selling withdrawal right. Neither exists: `EmbeddedCheckoutForm.tsx:23-34` POSTs only `{pack}`; no consent checkbox appears anywhere in `/checkout` or `/pricing`; `checkout.ts:188-196` grants the entitlement unconditionally.

*The sharper version:* it is not that the product lacks the data. `modules/auth/schemas.ts:6` sets `MIN_AGE = 14`, the register form collects `birthYear`, and `auth/service.ts:38,47` persists it to `User.birthYear`. A repo-wide grep shows **zero reads of `birthYear` anywhere outside registration**. The product knowingly accepts 14-year-old accounts and already stores the age needed to gate the purchase — it just never reads it. ### Delivery weight on a phone (H-10 … H-11)

**H-10 · Every mistake reel eagerly downloads a 1.1 MB PNG poster. Effort: 4h**
Measured live against the production build: creating exactly the element `MistakeMedia.tsx` renders (`preload="none"`, `poster=k2.png`) produced `transferSize = 1,122,531` for a poster while the 3 MB `.webm` stayed unfetched (`readyState 0`). The browser fetches posters regardless of `preload="none"`, so the IntersectionObserver lazy-load (`MistakeMedia.tsx:62-85`) — which genuinely works for the video — does not protect it.

42 poster PNGs = 47.2 MB, avg 1150.9 KB, displayed in a box clamped to 140–240 px height. All 210 keyframe PNGs = 237 MB, more than double the 103 MB of actual video. Recompressed with the project's own installed `sharp`: 1096 KB PNG → 48 KB jpeg q80 → **30 KB webp q78** → 15 KB webp at 854 px. That is a **36–74× overshoot**.

*Correction:* it affects **wrong answers only** — correct answers keep media behind a disclosure (`whyPanelModel.ts:206`), so realistic first-session cost is ~1.5–2 MB, not 5.5 MB. But coverage is worse than assumed: all 585 sim-ref questions resolve to a trace present in the manifest, so there is no 2D-fallback path that avoids the poster.

**Fix:** batch-convert fault keyframes to WebP (sharp, q78, 854 px wide) in `tools/clips/headless/render-clip.mjs`, write `.webp` paths into the manifest, return them from `posterFrameFor()`. 47.2 MB → ~1.3 MB.

**H-11 · Starting the simulator costs ~9.5 MB and 5.4 MB of JS to parse, with no connection awareness. Effort: 10h**
Byte-exact from the production build: rapier chunk **2,236,377 raw / 834,162 gz** (containing one contiguous 2,092,530-char base64 blob — `rapier3d-compat` inlines its wasm as a JS string); three.js/KTX2/Draco/postprocessing chunk **1,740,303 / 553,885**; simulator sync entry 14 chunks **1,562,200 / 373,390**. Plus KTX2 world textures 4,465,053 B, a 1.5 MB HDR mounted unconditionally at every quality level (`LessonScene.tsx:933-941`), `basis_transcoder.wasm` 515 KB + `draco_decoder.wasm` 279 KB, district JSON 176 KB. **≈ 9.8 MB.**

`grep -rn "saveData|effectiveType|navigator.connection|deviceMemory|hardwareConcurrency"` over `src/` returns **zero** non-test hits. The auto-quality heuristic cold-starts at "med" (shadows + full N8AO/bloom/SMAA composer) unless `dpr >= 3` — and this is unit-test-enforced (`quality.test.ts:130` asserts `dpr: 2 → "med"`); mid-range Androids report 2.625–2.75.

*Correction, important:* a phone-class **render** tier does exist and is deliberate (`QUALITY_PRESETS.low`: no shadows, no postprocessing, `maxDpr 1.0`, `facadeMaps: "colorOnly"`). Reporting "no mobile tier" would send you rebuilding something you already have.

**The real defect is sharper: `low` is a render tier only, never a download tier.**
1. `pbrTextures.ts:302-317` — `usePbrSet()` takes no quality argument and unconditionally loads color+normal+roughness+ao. Identical 4.47 MB at low, med and high.
2. `facadeTextures.ts:97-110,167-170` — downloads all four maps regardless of `facadeMaps`. At `"colorOnly"` the normal and ORM maps are fetched, transcoded, uploaded to VRAM and **never bound**. The tier explicitly labelled "phone-class" pays full freight for maps it discards.
3. The HDR sits outside any quality check.

Mobile is unambiguously in scope: `capabilities.ts:11-13` records that the old touch-refusal screen was *removed* as an ADR-005 violation. And the founder's own gate is an unchecked box — `docs/simulation/68:191`: "[ ] Runs on a mid-range Android phone: touch controls, 30+ fps median at tier-low, <10 s load". No .har, Lighthouse or trace artifact exists anywhere in the repo. **Treat "playable on a mid-range Android" as UNVERIFIED.**

**Fix, in payoff order:** (1) switch `@dimforge/rapier3d-compat` → `@dimforge/rapier3d` so the wasm ships as a real `.wasm` that stream-compiles instead of being decoded from a 2 MB JS string — largest cheap win, independent of tiering; (2) make `usePbrSet` and `useFacadeTextures` quality-aware and gate the HDR at `low`; (3) ship half-res normal maps for low/med (ground+road normals alone are 2.48 MB); (4) seed the cold-start recommendation from `hardwareConcurrency`/`deviceMemory`/`(pointer: coarse)` rather than dpr alone.

**H-12 · CI is RED at HEAD, and the deploy path bypasses CI entirely. Effort: 6h**

### Ops (H-17)

**H-17 · Auto-deploy deletes `.next` under the live process, with no health check and no rollback. Effort: 4h**
`/opt/knijka/autodeploy.sh` runs `git reset --hard` → `npm ci` → `prisma migrate deploy` → `rm -rf .next && npm run build` → `pm2 restart`, under `set -euo pipefail`. The old server keeps serving from the directory just deleted. Proven, not theoretical: `knijka-error.log` contains 6 × `ENOENT ... .next/required-server-files.json`; `/var/log/knijka-autodeploy.log` shows 31 `deploying` lines and 30 `deployed`. *Corrections, both making it worse:* the one aborted deploy failed at `pm2 restart` (unset `HOME`), not at build. Staging stayed broken from 23:03 on 07-19 until an unrelated commit at 10:24 on 07-20 — **~11.5 hours**, recoverable only by a new push. Autodeploy also matches on SHA alone and does not consult the CI check-run, so a red commit deploys anyway.

**Fix:** build to a temp dir and swap (`--distDir .next.new` → `mv`), with `trap` rollback; add a post-restart health gate (`curl -fsS localhost:3100/api/health` in a retry loop) — which requires adding a trivial `/api/health` route, as none of the 8 existing routes is one.

## 6. Innovation slate — what could make this exceptional

The highest-leverage innovations here are **unlocks of machinery that already exists**, not new construction. Four assets make them cheap:

1. **`ScenarioTrace`** — one recorded-kinematics shape serving the ghost, the mistake demo, the student's attempt and the offline renderer, with bit-stable playback guaranteed by design (`traces/types.ts`).
2. **The headless render pipeline** — `render-clip.mjs` drives the live app in headless Chromium on a real GPU, reads pixels off the WebGL backbuffer, enforces an R1 required-actor gate that *deletes* an output where the actor never framed, and upserts a manifest. ~58 s/clip, documented well enough to survive its author (`docs/development/69`).
3. **The rule catalog as a grounding corpus** — 52 violations each type-forced to carry severity, official points, `titleBg`, `explanationBg`, `correctiveBg`, `lawRef` and often a `conceptId`. A new code cannot ship without its corrective. This is what makes ADR-002 enforceable rather than aspirational — and it is currently under-consumed by the tutor.
4. **The scenario event library** — 45 events annotated with `examPointsCovered`, `coverageQuestions`, build complexity and safety criticality, ordered by exam-point leverage. It tells you which piece of 3D work buys the most exam coverage. Most projects at this stage are guessing.

**The slate.** Several of these rest on gap claims that were **refuted during verification** (Appendix A) — the *ideas* stand, the *enabling gaps* must be re-checked before scoping.

| Bet | What it is | Enabling asset | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| **I-1 · Feed the rule catalog to the tutor** | The tutor currently grounds on question/concept material. The 52-entry catalog is a second, richer grounded corpus with law refs and correctives already attached — retrieval-and-citation compatible with no ADR-002 change. Cheapest genuine capability gain in the product. | Rule catalog + existing citation whitelisting (`prompt.test.ts` proves invented markers are dropped) | Verified assets; scope ~16h |

| **I-3 · Render the remaining recorded mistake demos** | Rendering is a pure function of committed traces; ~58 s/clip, `--only-missing` path already exists. Whatever the true unrendered count, expanding reel coverage is the cheapest content expansion available. | headless pipeline + R0–R6 gate | **Count claim (268 of 310) refuted — re-derive from the manifest** |
| **I-4 · Parent-facing practical-exam protocol** | The sim already produces an official-format scored assessment (10/3/1, ≤9 total, ≤6 основни, no опасна) with server-rebuilt events. ## 8. Straight answers

**Is this launchable?**
No — but for reasons that are unusually cheap to fix. The engineering is not the problem: the typecheck is clean at 186k LOC, there is 1 TODO in all of `src/`, the ADRs are structurally enforced, and 6,845 deterministic tests run in 127 seconds. What is missing is a company (entity, mailbox, password reset, deletion), a paywall that gates what it sells, four rule-engine corrections, and one content-review pass. **~62 engineering hours plus ~30 hours of founder editorial work.** Nothing on the launch-blocker list requires an architectural change or a rewrite.

**What is the single biggest risk?**
Not any one finding — it is the **combination of C-1 and C-3: the product can begin taking money from minors by setting two environment variables, under a policy naming "[ИМЕ НА ЮРИДИЧЕСКО ЛИЦЕ]", selling two features that are already free.** No test fails, no CI turns red, no error is logged. That is a silent, unguarded transition from "harmless staging toy" to "consumer-law and GDPR exposure against 14-year-olds", and it is one env var away at all times.

The runner-up, and the one that damages the *product* rather than the company: **the rule engine instant-fails legally correct driving in at least four verified places on the shipped exam route** (C-4, H-5, H-6). A driving product that punishes the correct action at maximum severity, contradicting the sign in front of the student, destroys trust in every other verdict it will ever issue — including the ones that are right.

**What is the single highest-leverage thing to do next?**
Ship item **0.2** — the placeholder guard — today, in under an hour, before anything else. Not because it fixes anything, but because it makes the biggest risk in the product *structurally impossible to trigger by accident*. Then work Tier 0 in order.

If you want the highest-leverage thing that improves the *product* rather than protects it: **0.13, the position-bias check plus a seeded option shuffle across all 1,089 questions — 4 hours.** It permanently closes a defect that has already been hand-fixed twice and regenerated twice, it stops the readiness number (the thing the whole product sells) from being inflatable by a student who has learned nothing, and unlike almost everything else on this list it gets ~10× more expensive after content ships.
