# 66 · Produced-media ground rules (2026-07-21, founder-ratified after pilot v1)

Pilot v1 verdict: **19 of 20 clips wrong.** The founder's ruling, verbatim
intent: „we have to establish ground rules that can't be overpassed… this
can't be just simple, it must be done properly if we are about to do 400–500
and then expand." These rules are LAW for every produced visual artifact —
clips, stills, future reels. No compaction, session change, or agent may
un-know them. They extend doc 62's principles (P1–P6) and doc 64 THEO-4.

## The validity rules — an artifact may reach the founder ONLY if ALL hold

R1. **Every actor the mistake concerns is present and visible.** A
following-distance clip without the lead car, a yield clip without the
conflict car, an ambulance clip without the ambulance is not a simplified
artifact — it is a FALSE one. Invalid by definition.

R2. **The governing control is in frame before the fault.** The sign, light,
or marking that explains WHY must be visible to the viewer before the fault
happens (camera starts wider / orients toward it if needed). A stop without a
visible reason teaches nothing (founder v1: №9, №15).

R3. **The fault moment occurs inside the clip and is visually identifiable.**
"Nothing happens" clips (v1: №12, №16, №17) are invalid. The trim window must
be anchored to the REAL first-violation time (engine-computed, not guessed
from annotations).

R4. **Cabin-state faults show the cabin.** A missing seatbelt cannot be
filmed from behind the car (v1: №18). Per-mistake view selection: cockpit
view and/or the dashboard strip for belt/lights/wipers/gear faults; exterior
for road faults.

R5. **No rendering defects.** Wrong exposure/brightness, geometry clipping
(v1: №7 trees on the path), interpolation pops (№6 "hop"), floating objects —
each invalidates the artifact. Capture must reuse the drill's EXACT world
recipe — never a parallel reconstruction.

R6. **Something happens.** An empty road with a car driving straight is not a
mistake demonstration regardless of technical correctness.

## The enforcement rule — R0, above all others

R0. **Claude watches everything before the founder does.** Every produced
clip/still emits keyframes; Claude inspects them WITH VISION against R1–R6
and certifies each artifact, item by item. The founder reviews only what
passed Claude's watch — the founder's review is taste and truth, never
defect-hunting. "Tests green" and "file exists" are NOT evidence for visual
work; only looking is. (Root cause of v1: nobody looked.)

## Per-clip requirements card

Every clip carries a machine-derived card: required actors (from the
template's staged spec), the governing control (from the map/zone/sign data),
required view (cabin vs exterior, from the mistake's codes), and the
engine-computed fault time. The card is Claude's R0 checklist and renders in
the review gallery so the founder sees WHAT the clip was required to show.

## Scale gate

Mass production (the ~450) starts ONLY after a pilot generation passes both
gates: R0 (Claude's certification, every clip) and the founder's taste pass.
A failed pilot costs one evening; these rules exist so it never costs a
month.
